Okapi for Android, in early development

Sign in to the same site twice.

Okapi keeps separate Profiles, and each one has its own cookies, Tabs, history, bookmarks and downloads. Work mail in one Profile, personal mail in another, both signed in. Nothing in one Profile is visible from another.

The browser wears the color of the Profile you're in. Pick one for this page.

What a Profile keeps to itself

Each Profile is its own session context inside GeckoView, Mozilla's web engine, with its own cookies and storage on disk.

Kept per Profile

  • Cookies and site storage
  • Cache and site permissions
  • Service workers
  • Tabs and Pinned Tabs
  • History, up to the latest 5,000 visits
  • Bookmarks and Folders
  • The list of downloads
  • Site icons
  • A name, a color and an avatar

Shared by all Profiles

  • Search engine
  • Suggestions on or off
  • Safe Browsing on or off
  • Theme: device, light or dark
  • Site Rules
  • Compact rows

A Profile's web data store is named after its ID, never its name. Rename a Profile and you stay signed in. Delete it and its data is wiped at the next cold start (ADR 0004).

Moving between Profiles

A Tab can go to another Profile, or its page can open there while the Tab stays put.

Move and Open in Profile

Move takes a Tab to another Profile. The original Tab closes, and the page reloads under that Profile's Accounts. Open in Profile does the same and keeps the original Tab. Both live in the page menu.

Goes with the Tab

  • Its current address

Stays behind

  • Back/forward history
  • Title and scroll position
  • Unsaved form input (you're warned first)

The history belongs to the Profile the Tab came from. Carrying it over would leak one Profile's browsing into another (ADR 0003).

Incoming Links and Site Rules

Make Okapi your default browser and a link from another app opens as a new Tab in the Active Profile, the one you were last using. If that's the wrong one, "Move to Profile…" is one tap away on the page.

A Site Rule decides for you: links for that site always open in the Profile you chose. After you Move a link, the page offers "Always" to make the rule in one tap.

How a Site Rule matches
RuleCoversDoesn't cover
example.com example.com, www.example.com, mail.example.com example.org
example.com/work /work, /work/today /workshop
*.example.com Only the sites under example.com example.com itself

"Check a link" under Settings → Site Rules tells you which Profile a link would open in. Incoming Links never open as Private Tabs, and only http and https links get in.

Tabs, as each Profile keeps them

Pinned Tabs and Folders

A Profile's bookmarks are its Pinned Tabs. They sit under "Pinned" at the top of the Tab list, open or closed. Closing one closes its Tab and keeps the bookmark. Group them in Folders, one level deep, and hold a row to drag it into place.

Private Tabs

Every Profile can open Private Tabs. They share a temporary store apart from the Profile's own and write no history. Screenshots are blocked while one shows. Close the last one, or the app's window, and they end. Their cookies and site storage are cleared.

Tabs that come back

When Android kills the app, each normal Tab returns with its address, title and scroll position. Private Tabs don't. At most 5 Tabs stay live across all Profiles. The rest reload when you open them.

A color per Profile

Pick one of 12 colors, soft, medium or strong, and an icon or any emoji for the avatar. The Sidebar, Bottom Bar and Tab Overview take that color, so you can tell which Profile you're in.

History, bookmarks, downloads

All three are per Profile. A page can only offer a file. It downloads after you agree, with that Profile's cookies. Each row of the Tab list shows its site's icon, kept per Profile too.

Search and suggestions

Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo or Brave Search. Suggestions come from the Profile's bookmarks and history first. An address or text shaped like credentials never goes to the search engine, and suggestion requests carry no cookies.

Two layouts, picked by window width

The layout follows the width of the window, not the kind of device. It switches live when you rotate, split the screen or unfold, and your Tabs stay put.

No telemetry, no Google Play Services

A browser sees everything you do online. So Okapi has no analytics, no crash reporter and no service that needs a key (ADR 0002).

  • No telemetry. No analytics and no crash-reporting SDK, in the app or in its engine. Crash reports come from people who open a GitHub issue and paste a log they've read. The privacy policy says what the engine itself sends.
  • No Google Play Services. No Firebase, no API keys. A public repository with no secrets in the app has no secret to leak.
  • Sessions stay on the device. Cloud backup and device-to-device transfer leave out all of the app's data, so your signed-in sessions are never copied off the device.
  • Quiet logs. Release builds never log addresses, page titles or cookies.
  • Safe Browsing without a key. It's built into GeckoView, so the app doesn't need one.
  • Built in public, MIT licensed. The code, the spec and the architecture decisions, with the reasons behind them, are on GitHub.

This page follows the same rule: no fonts, scripts or trackers from other servers.

  • While a Private Tab shows, screenshots and the recent-apps preview are blocked. In the Tab list it carries a lock.
  • A Private Tab can't be Moved or opened in another Profile, and never takes an Incoming Link.

not merged The name Okapi
  • The browser is now called Okapi. JRNY stays the maker's name. The launcher shows "Okapi", and the Google Play title will be "Okapi Browser by JRNY". The application ID stays app.jrny.browser, so an installed copy updates in place and keeps its Profiles. ADR 0005 says why.
not merged GeckoView, Mozilla's engine, renders pages
  • Pages render in GeckoView inside the app instead of Android System WebView. Sites see Firefox for Android, and no request carries the app's package name any more.
  • Page dialogs, list and date fields, Okapi's own error pages, and downloads written by Okapi with their progress on the list.
  • Deleting a Profile wipes its web data at once. Private Tabs keep their storage in memory only.

not merged Fixed: suggestions and passwords typed before the @
  • While you type, text with a colon before its first /, ? or #, such as admin:hunter2, is no longer sent to the search engine for suggestions, because an @ typed next would make it credentials. Before, a pause before the @ could send the password.
  • A search with an early colon, such as python: list comprehension, now gets only bookmark and history suggestions while you type. Go still searches for it.

Known limits

  • Passkeys don't work until Okapi is approved as a privileged browser. Password managers work through Android Autofill.
  • There is no Custom Tabs service. An app that asks for a Custom Tab gets a normal Tab.
  • A download stops if Android ends the app while it runs.
  • Back/forward history isn't restored after Android kills the app.
  • A Move loses unsaved form input and pages reached by submitting a form.
  • Sign-in with two Accounts at once still needs checking on GeckoView, for Google, Microsoft, GitHub and Apple.
  • Not in v1: sync, extensions, a built-in password manager, content blocking and a lock per Profile.

After v1

On the roadmap once the first release is out. None of it is built yet.
